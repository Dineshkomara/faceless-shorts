# Shared Production Quality Standard (English + Telugu)

This standard applies to every scheduled and manual production on both channels. Keep their established topic boundaries, language, and story traditions independent. It overrides older prompt instructions that demand one fixed shot count, camera move, sound cue, cast, music level, or visual recipe.

When a non-default visual style is selected, read its dedicated profile and preserve its defining art direction exactly for that run. The selected style may supersede conflicting generic renderer recommendations below, but it does not weaken the shared evidence, cleanliness, shot-coverage, audio, asset-library, export, or strict feedback gates. Never mutate another style's profile or apply an English-only style to Telugu.

## Scene and camera direction

- Before any renderer code, write `review/production_board.md`. Treat it as the detailed production prompt for the model: state the premise/claim map and locked visual style, then write one self-contained shot card for every stable shot ID. Each card includes narration/time, purpose, set/location/time, all characters and their complete locked appearance, start pose/expression, timed actions and expression changes, scale/angle/camera path, foreground/mid/background and parallax, motivated light source/color/spill, optional atmosphere, exact in-scene graphics and timing, captions-safe area, sound cue/music change, continuity handoff, and relevant positive/negative directions. A reader should be able to reconstruct the start frame and full shot without guessing. Do not rely on “same character”, “same location”, generic “cinematic”, or ungrounded model judgment; repeat the necessary details in each card. Store a character reference block, set/color continuity notes, visual negative rules, source-to-claim map, shot-type tally, and asset-library decisions in the same board.
- Plan multiple timed visual events inside a six-second beat (for example, a camera drift plus a gesture/expression change or a diagram reveal). The shot can run longer or shorter when narration needs it. Use a motivated scene-to-detail cut, zoom/reveal, pan between subjects, or reaction cut when it clarifies cause/effect; continuity and legibility take priority over forced cuts. Encode the direction as actual deterministic animation and camera/action timing in the renderer, not just in the board.
- Design the video as a sequence of distinct, purposeful shots. Target 18–24 shots for a 60–90 second video and 22–30 for a 90–150 second video. Most shots should last 2–4 seconds; an animated explanation can hold up to 6 seconds when meaningful motion continues. Add inserts and angle changes within long narration sentences; do not add filler or rush the narration to hit a shot count.
- Before code, write a shot list with one visual action per shot, its set, scale, camera angle/move, and narration beat. Cover at least four scales/angles across the edit: establishing wide, medium action, close detail, and a changed viewpoint such as overhead, low angle, profile, cross-section, or over-shoulder. Avoid consecutive shots that reuse the same composition.
- Build 3–5 topic-specific set-pieces for each video (for example: map/site, main environment, close-up mechanism, cutaway/assembly, finished hero view). Reuse the designed world, but move to new places within it and change the camera side/height/scale. Do not repeat one hero object from slightly different distances for most of the runtime.
- Give important objects designed shapes and materials: readable silhouettes, layered surfaces, bevels/edges, seams, fasteners or structural joints where appropriate, restrained roughness/metal/glass response, contact shadows, and believable scale cues. Use soft three-point lighting and atmospheric depth. Prefer coherent stylized realism over random low-poly filler, glossy plastic, excessive bokeh, or indiscriminate particle effects.
- Model the subject's defining shape faithfully before adding decoration. For architecture, use a continuous roof or dome surface when the real structure is continuous; use rings or blocks only when they explain the actual build sequence. Include one readable scale cue, the important junctions, and a clear silhouette. Keep stone, timber, water, and metal visually distinct. Avoid overexposed white surfaces and reduce bloom until texture and edges remain visible on a phone.
- Plan at least five visibly distinct compositions across a full video: establishing wide, medium action, close detail, changed viewpoint (interior/overhead/cross-section/profile), and a payoff hero angle. Change set-piece or viewpoint when the explanation changes. Do not reuse the same hero composition for adjacent explanation beats.
- Stage one main action at a time. Show cause before effect and keep geometry/flow/load paths consistent across cuts. Animate assembly, transformation, character gesture, flow, or reveal when the narration names it; make cuts and captions reinforce that beat.
- Captions are the only persistent text overlay. Add at most one temporary label or diagram label when it teaches a necessary fact; never repeat the spoken caption as a banner. Remove persistent HUD panels, repeated chapter bars, decorative badges, floating particles, duplicate titles, and props that do not explain the current idea. Keep one dominant focal action and clear negative space in each frame, with no competing overlay pair. If the frame reads as busy at phone size, simplify it before rendering. Recheck every remaining overlay for safe-area and caption collisions.

## Narration and mix

- Write in natural spoken language. Use complete, connected sentences with contractions/conversational links in English and idiomatic spoken Telugu in Telugu. Avoid fragments that sound like a list, repeated rhetorical questions, fake urgency, forced dialogue, and over-explaining the on-screen text.
- Split a sentence into multiple visual cut lines without adding sentence-ending punctuation until the spoken sentence is complete. This lets the TTS perform a full sentence in one breath while the visuals can cut every 2–4 seconds. Use commas and natural pauses; retain punctuation that guides intonation. Set normal sentence gaps around 0.24–0.32 s and an outro pause around 0.6–0.9 s.
- Use one stable, native-language narrator by default: English neural voice at 0% rate and neutral pitch; Telugu `te-IN-MohanNeural` or configured Sarvam narrator at natural pace and neutral pitch. Pick a different narrator only when it clearly fits the channel. Use dialogue voices only when characters genuinely speak in the source story; keep pitch/rate changes subtle and leave natural reaction gaps. Never manufacture an ensemble for a documentary explanation.
- Read technical names aloud before committing; add phonetic guidance only when needed. Write for one natural breath and a conversational cadence, not a list of caption fragments. Prefer a suitable locally available neural voice when a remote TTS service fails; do not repeat failing network calls indefinitely.
- Keep the narrator clearly in front. Use an evolving music bed with a restrained motif rather than a bright repeated arpeggio. Duck it smoothly under speech. Use only a few soft, scene-matched effects at meaningful actions; do not put a whoosh/riser/impact on every cut or stack effects with speech. Every cue type emitted into `data/cues.json` must map to an implemented sound in `tools/mix.py`; fail the audio review if any cue is silently ignored. Preserve pauses and consonants; avoid audible pumping, hard clipping, heavy echo, or hyped bass/treble.
- Listen to the rendered mix as well as checking it numerically. Record integrated loudness and true peak with FFmpeg EBU R128 analysis; aim near -14 LUFS integrated and no higher than -1 dBTP. Do not treat sample-peak normalization alone as loudness compliance: measure, apply gain toward -14 LUFS, then confirm the true peak still stays at or below -1 dBTP after AAC encoding. Listen for voice masking, harsh transients, dead silence, or music that becomes tiring over the full runtime.

## Automated review gate

- The production board is mandatory for both channels and must identify every stable shot ID in the renderer. The QA feedback gate checks for this board and its shot-ID coverage before a video can stage.
- Run the JavaScript syntax check, scene/check and pacing gate before rendering. Generate a contact sheet and inspect the opening, one frame from every major scene/set-piece, the busiest text frame, and the ending; inspect at least one full-size Telugu caption frame and the densest overlay frame.
- Fix repeated compositions, blocked/missing actions, subtitle or overlay collisions, and inaccurate construction/story continuity before rendering. After rendering, verify duration, 9:16 frame size, H.264/AAC tracks, that narration reaches the ending, and that the opening and payoff frames remain readable.
- Record any remaining material quality issue in the project ledger. A render is not ready to stage if the audio is distorted/clipped, the visual sequence repeats one set-piece, or a major label is unreadable.
